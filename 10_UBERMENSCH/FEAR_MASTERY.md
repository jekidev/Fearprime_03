# FearPrime × Übermensch: fear mastery

Within this project, the Übermensch framing is used as a **self-mastery model**, not as a diagnostic or biological claim.

## Core distinction

The target is not becoming incapable of fear.

A system with zero fear would lose useful information. The stronger target is:

- perceive threat accurately
- tolerate activation without automatic submission to it
- choose behavior deliberately
- update predictions when evidence changes
- retain defensive capability when danger is real
- recover efficiently after activation

## Reframing PTSD functions

Instead of asking only:

> How do we remove this symptom?

FearPrime also asks:

> What problem is this response trying to solve?

Then:

> Can we preserve the useful function while reducing false positives, rigidity and cost?

This reframes hypervigilance from simply “bad arousal” into a threat-detection system whose sensitivity/specificity can be studied. Avoidance becomes a risk-control policy whose short-term benefit and long-term learning cost can be measured.

## Mastery dimensions

1. **Detection** — notice real danger.
2. **Discrimination** — separate danger from resemblance-to-danger.
3. **Approach** — voluntarily enter safe situations despite conditioned activation.
4. **Prediction updating** — replace obsolete expectations when evidence contradicts them.
5. **Interoceptive control** — tolerate bodily fear signals without automatically interpreting them as catastrophe.
6. **Context flexibility** — retrieve safety learning outside the training context.
7. **Recovery** — return toward baseline efficiently after activation.
8. **Metacognitive ownership** — experience a defensive state as information, not an unquestionable command.

## FearPrime hypothesis

PTSD may sometimes be better modeled as **overprotective learning and control** than as a global deficiency. That hypothesis does not imply every symptom is beneficial or that all threat interpretations are accurate. Its value is that it generates testable questions about calibration, generalization, prediction and control.
