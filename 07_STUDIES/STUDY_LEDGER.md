# FearPrime study ledger

This is the canonical evidence ledger. Entries marked VERIFIED have had title/PMID/DOI/design details checked against primary or PubMed-indexed sources during the 2026-09-15 FearPrime_03 reconstruction.

## Evidence codes

- A = human clinical outcome evidence
- B = human experimental fear-learning evidence
- C = translational/preclinical evidence
- D = mechanistic/receptor evidence
- E = FearPrime hypothesis

## Sodium butyrate / HDAC

### VERIFIED — Ribbens et al. 2026

**Title:** The HDAC inhibitor sodium butyrate moderately enhances long-term fear extinction memory retrieval in humans  
**Journal:** Molecular Psychiatry  
**Published:** 2026-08-05  
**PMID:** 42557391  
**DOI:** 10.1038/s41380-026-03802-1  
**Design:** randomized, triple-blind, placebo-controlled, six-arm human experimental study  
**N:** 180 healthy participants  
**Study dose:** single 4 g oral sodium butyrate administration (study metadata, not a FearPrime dosing recommendation)  
**Timing:** before or immediately after extinction; short versus long extinction manipulation  
**Delayed test:** day 9 / roughly seven days after extinction

**Main result:** NaBu improved later extinction-memory retrieval on expectancy outcomes when extinction learning was sufficiently robust. It did not prevent reinstatement, and delayed SCR did not show the same treatment effect.

**FearPrime interpretation:** the strongest current translational support for the idea that HDAC/plasticity modulation can strengthen retrieval of well-acquired extinction learning. It does not replace exposure and did not erase return-of-fear.

**Evidence:** B  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42557391/

### VERIFIED — rat PTSD-model study

**Title:** Sodium butyrate enhances fear extinction and rescues hippocampal acetylcholinesterase activity in a rat model of posttraumatic stress disorder  
**PMID:** 33883448

**Main result:** NaBu reversed extinction deficits in a single-prolonged-stress model and changed hippocampal HDAC/acetylcholinesterase measures.

**Evidence:** C  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33883448/

### VERIFIED — HDAC review

**Title:** HDAC inhibitors as cognitive enhancers in fear, anxiety and trauma therapy: where do we stand?  
**PMID:** 24646280

**FearPrime relevance:** reviews preclinical evidence that increased histone acetylation can strengthen long-term extinction memory and discusses BDNF/NMDA-related pathways.

**Evidence:** C/D  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24646280/

## Dopamine / L-DOPA

### VERIFIED — Haaker, Lonsdorf & Kalisch 2015

**Title:** Effects of post-extinction l-DOPA administration on the spontaneous recovery and reinstatement of fear in a human fMRI study  
**Journal:** European Neuropsychopharmacology  
**PMID:** 26238968  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.euroneuro.2015.07.016  
**Design:** human cue/context fear conditioning and extinction; L-DOPA versus placebo after extinction  
**Delayed test:** one week later

**Main result:** no clear drug effect on conditioned skin conductance at return-of-fear testing, but the L-DOPA group showed reduced neural activity in fear/return-of-fear-associated regions and increased vmPFC activity relative to placebo.

**FearPrime interpretation:** supports a dopaminergic consolidation/retrieval hypothesis, but behavioral evidence is incomplete and requires replication.

**Evidence:** B  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26238968/

### VERIFIED — paradigm paper

**Title:** Multimodal assessment of long-term memory recall and reinstatement in a combined cue and context fear conditioning and extinction paradigm in humans  
**PMID:** 24116095  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24116095/

## Oxytocin

### VERIFIED — Eckstein et al. 2015

**Title:** Oxytocin facilitates the extinction of conditioned fear in humans  
**Journal:** Biological Psychiatry  
**PMID:** 25542304  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.biopsych.2014.10.015  
**Design:** randomized double-blind placebo-controlled fMRI experiment  
**N:** 62 healthy men

**Main result:** oxytocin altered prefrontal/amygdala responses, increased early extinction electrodermal responding, and enhanced decline later in extinction.

**Evidence:** B  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25542304/

### VERIFIED — Acheson et al. 2013

**Title:** The effect of intranasal oxytocin treatment on conditioned fear extinction and recall in a healthy human sample  
**PMID:** 23644911  
**Design:** double-blind placebo-controlled experiment  
**N:** 44 healthy participants

**Main result:** increased early fear-potentiated startle during extinction, similar end-of-extinction reduction, but better next-day extinction recall in the oxytocin group.

**Evidence:** B  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23644911/

### VERIFIED — Rashidi et al. 2025

**Title:** Effects of intranasal oxytocin on fear extinction learning  
**Journal:** Neuropsychopharmacology  
**PMID:** 39313675  
**Design:** randomized double-blind placebo-controlled two-day study  
**N:** 124 healthy naturally cycling women

**Main result:** oxytocin impaired extinction learning in the tested conditions, including administration before extinction.

**FearPrime interpretation:** falsifies any simple universal "oxytocin = extinction enhancer" model; timing/context/sex/task variables must be modeled explicitly.

**Evidence:** B  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39313675/

### VERIFIED — fear consolidation

**Title:** Influence of intranasal oxytocin on fear consolidation in healthy humans  
**PMID:** 31922086  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31922086/

## NMDA / D-cycloserine

### VERIFIED — Norberg, Krystal & Tolin 2008

**Title:** A meta-analysis of D-cycloserine and the facilitation of fear extinction and exposure therapy  
**Journal:** Biological Psychiatry  
**PMID:** 18313643  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.biopsych.2008.01.012

**Main result:** early literature supported DCS as an extinction/exposure augmenter, with possible effects on speed/efficiency and diminishing gains over repeated use.

**Evidence:** A/B synthesis  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18313643/

### VERIFIED — McGuire et al. 2017

**Title:** A Meta-Analysis of D-Cycloserine in Exposure-Based Treatment: Moderators of Treatment Efficacy, Response, and Diagnostic Remission  
**PMID:** 27314661  
**DOI:** 10.4088/JCP.15r10334  
**Included trials:** 20 randomized placebo-controlled trials, 957 participants across anxiety disorders/OCD/PTSD

**Main result:** small, generally nonsignificant overall augmentation effects with heterogeneity.

**FearPrime interpretation:** a key warning that a plausible plasticity mechanism does not guarantee large clinical augmentation.

**Evidence:** A  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27314661/

### VERIFIED — PTSD moderator paper

**Title:** Extinction learning as a moderator of d-cycloserine efficacy for enhancing exposure therapy in posttraumatic stress disorder  
**PMID:** 26121495

**Main result:** extinction learning related to outcome, but a consistent DCS-by-extinction-learning moderation effect was not demonstrated.

**Evidence:** A/B  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26121495/

## Lurasidone / Latuda

### VERIFIED pharmacology — not direct extinction evidence

**Pharmacological profile of lurasidone, a novel antipsychotic agent with potent 5-HT7 and 5-HT1A receptor activity**  
PMID: 20404009  
DOI: 10.1124/jpet.110.167346  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20404009/

**Pharmacokinetics and Pharmacodynamics of Lurasidone Hydrochloride: A Systematic Review**  
PMID: 27722855  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27722855/

**Update on the development of lurasidone as a treatment for patients with acute schizophrenia**  
PMID: 22675261  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22675261/

**Lurasidone: The 2016 update on the pharmacology, efficacy and safety profile**  
PMID: 27203278  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27203278/

**Verified receptor profile relevant to FearPrime:** D2 and 5-HT2A antagonism; high-affinity 5-HT7 antagonism; 5-HT1A partial agonism. Preclinical literature reports BDNF-related and NMDA-function hypotheses.

**Direct-evidence audit:** no direct human fear-extinction/PTSD exposure-augmentation evidence for lurasidone was established in this literature pass.

**FearPrime status:** D/E — mechanistic hypothesis, not an evidence-backed extinction protocol.

PMID: 9218165

PMID: 8996184

## Reconsolidation comparator track — propranolol

### VERIFIED — conflicting meta-analytic conclusions retained

**Effects of propranolol on the modification of trauma memory reconsolidation in PTSD patients: a systematic review and meta-analysis**  
PMID: 35405409  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35405409/

**Impairing memory reconsolidation with propranolol in healthy and clinical samples: a meta-analysis**  
PMID: 35361699  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35361699/

**Updated and rectified meta-analysis shows no effect of propranolol versus placebo on traumatic memory reconsolidation disruption**  
PMID: 36130812  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36130812/

**FearPrime interpretation:** conflicting syntheses are preserved deliberately. Reconsolidation effects depend on design/boundary conditions and should not be described as established "memory erasure."
