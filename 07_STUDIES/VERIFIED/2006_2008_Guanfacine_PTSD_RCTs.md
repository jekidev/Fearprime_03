# Guanfacine in chronic PTSD — two negative veteran trials

**Status:** VERIFIED HUMAN CLINICAL RCT EVIDENCE  
**Evidence grade:** A/B  
**Track:** alpha-2A / noradrenergic regulation

## Neylan et al. 2006
*No improvement of posttraumatic stress disorder symptoms with guanfacine treatment.*

- PMID: 17151174
- DOI: 10.1176/appi.ajp.163.12.2186
- N = 63 veterans with chronic PTSD.
- Guanfacine n = 29; placebo n = 34.
- 8-week double-blind randomized controlled trial.
- Started at 0.5 mg and increased in weekly 0.5 mg increments to a target 1–3 mg at bedtime.
- Mean dose reported ~2.4 mg.

### Result
No advantage over placebo for PTSD symptoms, subjective sleep quality or general mood. Guanfacine produced several side effects.

Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17151174/

## Davis et al. 2008
*A placebo-controlled trial of guanfacine for the treatment of posttraumatic stress disorder in veterans.*

- PMID: 18362867
- 8-week double-blind placebo-controlled trial followed by open-label extension.
- Predominantly male combat-veteran sample.

### Result
No significant drug-versus-placebo differences on clinician-rated or self-reported PTSD outcomes.

Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18362867/

## FearPrime interpretation
The mechanistic argument for alpha-2A signaling — reducing excessive noradrenergic tone and potentially improving prefrontal control — remains biologically plausible.

But existing adult chronic-veteran PTSD trials do **not** support guanfacine as a general PTSD treatment, and these trials did not establish it as an extinction enhancer.

## Important distinction
Symptom-treatment failure does not prove that alpha-2A manipulation cannot affect a tightly timed extinction paradigm. It means the current direct clinical evidence is negative.

## FearPrime weight
**Negative clinical evidence; mechanistic research candidate only.**
