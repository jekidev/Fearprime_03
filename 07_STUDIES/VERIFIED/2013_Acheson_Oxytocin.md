# Acheson et al. 2013 — Oxytocin and extinction recall

**Status:** VERIFIED PRIMARY HUMAN STUDY  
**Evidence grade:** B  
**Population:** healthy humans

## Citation
Acheson D, Feifel D, de Wilde S, McKinney R, Lohr J, Risbrough V. *The effect of intranasal oxytocin treatment on conditioned fear extinction and recall in a healthy human sample.* Psychopharmacology. 2013;229(1):199-208.

- PMID: 23644911
- PMCID: PMC5458114
- DOI: 10.1007/s00213-013-3099-4
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23644911/

## Design
- Double-blind placebo-controlled study.
- N = 44 healthy participants.
- Fear acquisition first.
- Oxytocin/placebo after acquisition and before extinction.
- Extinction recall tested 24 h later.

## Intervention as studied
- 24 IU intranasal oxytocin.
- Extinction began ~45 min after administration.

## Main result
Compared with placebo:
- oxytocin produced **more fear-potentiated startle early in extinction**;
- by late extinction, groups were similar;
- 24 h later, the oxytocin group showed better extinction recall.

## FearPrime interpretation
Oxytocin cannot be treated as a simple acute calming agent. This study suggests a pattern in which early salience/fear can increase while later extinction retrieval improves.

## Debug / limitations
- Small healthy sample.
- Laboratory fear, not PTSD treatment.
- Later, larger human work reported impaired extinction learning with the same common 24-IU dose.

## FearPrime weight
**Positive but conflicted evidence.** Must be interpreted with Rashidi et al. 2025.
