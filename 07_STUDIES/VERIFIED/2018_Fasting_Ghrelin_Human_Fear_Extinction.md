# Overnight fasting / ghrelin and human fear extinction

**Status:** VERIFIED HUMAN EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE  
**Evidence grade:** B  
**Track:** metabolic state / ghrelin / extinction retention

## Citation
Shi L, Deng J, Chen S, et al. *Fasting enhances extinction retention and prevents the return of fear in humans.* Translational Psychiatry. 2018;8:214.

- PMID: 30301955
- DOI: 10.1038/s41398-018-0260-1
- N = 125 male participants
- Randomized fasting vs food condition

Primary:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30301955/
Journal:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-018-0260-1

## Main result
One-night fasting:
- did not materially alter initial fear acquisition;
- improved extinction retention;
- reduced return of fear;
- showed effects reported to persist for at least 6 months;
- was also tested with remote fear-memory procedures.

Fasting increased plasma ghrelin. Higher ghrelin was negatively associated with fear response during spontaneous-recovery testing.

Cortisol was not materially altered by the fasting manipulation.

## FearPrime interpretation

This is unusually direct evidence that **metabolic state can alter extinction retention** in humans.

A candidate pathway is:

fasting → increased ghrelin / metabolic-state shift → altered extinction consolidation or retrieval

but the experiment does not prove ghrelin is the sole causal mediator.

## Important distinction
This study tested **overnight fasting in healthy men**, not prolonged fasting, not repeated multi-day fasting, and not PTSD treatment.

The result cannot be extrapolated linearly to:

longer fasting = stronger extinction

## Debug
Potential mechanisms include ghrelin, arousal, glucose/insulin changes, catecholamines, attention, expectancy or other metabolic signals.

## FearPrime weight
**High experimental interest; clinical PTSD translation absent.**
