# Cannabidiol (CBD) — human extinction and exposure evidence

**Status:** VERIFIED MIXED HUMAN EVIDENCE  
**Evidence grade:** B  
**Track:** endocannabinoids / CBD

## Das et al. 2013 — experimental fear extinction

**Title:** Cannabidiol enhances consolidation of explicit fear extinction in humans

- PMID: 23307069
- DOI: 10.1007/s00213-012-2955-y
- N = 48 healthy participants
- Double-blind placebo-controlled fear-conditioning study
- 32 mg CBD administered before or after extinction
- Recall/reinstatement tested 48 h later

### Result
Post-extinction CBD improved **explicit shock-expectancy extinction consolidation**. There was no acute enhancement of within-session extinction. Physiological outcomes were less decisive, with only trend-level effects on some reinstatement measures.

Primary:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23307069/

## Kwee et al. 2022 — exposure-therapy RCT

**Title:** Cannabidiol enhancement of exposure therapy in treatment refractory patients with social anxiety disorder and panic disorder with agoraphobia: A randomised controlled trial

- PMID: 35561538
- N = 80 randomized
- CBD n = 39; placebo n = 41
- 300 mg oral CBD before eight weekly therapist-assisted in-vivo exposure sessions

### Result
CBD **did not improve clinical exposure-therapy outcomes** relative to placebo through treatment or 3- and 6-month follow-up.

Primary:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35561538/

## Fear-conditioning sub-study — 2023

**Title:** Effects of cannabidiol on fear conditioning in anxiety disorders: decreased threat expectation during retention, but no enhanced fear re-extinction

- PMID: 38044339
- N = 69 patients with panic disorder/agoraphobia or social anxiety disorder
- 300 mg CBD/placebo exposure augmentation

### Result
CBD reduced shock expectancy during retention under low/ambiguous threat but did **not** enhance fear re-extinction. In antidepressant users, some exploratory outcomes suggested impaired re-extinction/safety learning.

Primary:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38044339/

## PTSD trauma-recall study

**Title:** Effects of cannabidiol on symptoms induced by the recall of traumatic events in patients with posttraumatic stress disorder

- PMID: 35029706
- N = 33 PTSD participants
- CBD 300 mg vs placebo before trauma recall

This study examined acute responses and possible reconsolidation-related effects rather than standard extinction augmentation.

Primary:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35029706/

## FearPrime conclusion

The correct model is not:
`CBD = fear extinction enhancer`

Instead:
`CBD effect = endpoint × dose/formulation × timing × population × concomitant medication × learning paradigm`

A small early healthy-volunteer study found improved explicit consolidation, while a substantially more clinically relevant exposure RCT found **no augmentation of treatment outcome**.

## FearPrime weight
**Mixed. Do not promote CBD to core enhancer status.**
