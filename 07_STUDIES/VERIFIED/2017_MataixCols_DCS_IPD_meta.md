# Mataix-Cols et al. 2017 — D-cycloserine exposure-therapy IPD meta-analysis

**Status:** VERIFIED HUMAN META-ANALYSIS  
**Evidence grade:** A-/B+  
**Role:** benchmark pharmacological exposure-augmentation evidence

## Citation
Mataix-Cols D, Fernández de la Cruz L, Monzani B, et al. *D-Cycloserine Augmentation of Exposure-Based Cognitive Behavior Therapy for Anxiety, Obsessive-Compulsive, and Posttraumatic Stress Disorders: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Individual Participant Data.* JAMA Psychiatry. 2017;74(5):501-510.

- PMID: 28122091
- DOI: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2016.3955
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28122091/

## Dataset
- 21 of 22 eligible double-blind randomized trials contributed individual participant data.
- 1,047 of 1,073 eligible participants were included.
- Diagnoses included specific phobia, social anxiety disorder, panic disorder, OCD and PTSD.

## Main result
Compared with placebo augmentation, DCS was associated with a **small** improvement from pre- to post-treatment (reported d ≈ -0.25), with weaker/no clearly significant advantage at some other timepoints.

Antidepressant use did not significantly moderate the effect.

## FearPrime interpretation
DCS provides an important cautionary precedent:
- a mechanistically plausible learning enhancer can show only a small average clinical effect;
- effects depend on design, timing and likely the learning content of the exposure session;
- acute exposure outcomes, post-treatment outcomes and follow-up should be kept separate.

## Debug
- Heterogeneous disorders, protocols, dose schedules and outcome measures.
- Individual session learning quality could not be fully modeled in this pooled analysis.
- A mean effect does not imply universal enhancement.

## FearPrime weight
**High as a benchmark**, not because DCS is necessarily a primary FearPrime candidate, but because its translational history is highly informative for evaluating butyrate, L-DOPA and other enhancers.
