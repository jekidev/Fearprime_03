# Smits et al. 2013 — DCS can amplify good or bad exposure outcomes

**Status:** VERIFIED CLINICAL HUMAN ANALYSIS  
**Evidence grade:** B  
**Population:** adults with acrophobia

## Citation
Smits JAJ, Rosenfield D, Otto MW, et al. *D-Cycloserine Enhancement of Fear Extinction is Specific to Successful Exposure Sessions: Evidence from the Treatment of Height Phobia.* Biological Psychiatry. 2013;73(11):1054-1058.

- PMID: 23332511
- PMCID: PMC3636175
- DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsych.2012.12.009
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23332511/

## Design
- N = 29 medication-free adults with height phobia.
- Two 30-minute virtual-reality exposure sessions.
- Placebo n = 14.
- DCS n = 15.
- 50 mg DCS or placebo immediately after each exposure session.

## Main result
The effect of DCS depended on the state at the end of exposure:
- when end fear was low, DCS predicted greater improvement;
- when end fear remained high, DCS predicted worse improvement relative to placebo.

## FearPrime interpretation
This is an important clinical analogue of the Gerlicher L-DOPA boundary condition.

A learning enhancer does not inherently encode “safety.” It may strengthen whichever association/state dominates the session.

## Debug
This was a small reanalysis rather than a large independently powered trial designed around the moderator. End-fear is also an imperfect proxy for learning quality; inhibitory-learning models emphasize expectancy violation and new learning rather than simple within-session fear reduction.

## FearPrime weight
**Core conceptual precedent** for the rule: never equate “more plasticity” with “better outcome.”
