# Rashidi et al. 2025 — Oxytocin impaired extinction learning

**Status:** VERIFIED PRIMARY HUMAN RCT  
**Evidence grade:** B  
**Population:** healthy naturally cycling women

## Citation
Rashidi M, Simon JJ, Bertsch K, et al. *Effects of intranasal oxytocin on fear extinction learning.* Neuropsychopharmacology. 2025;50(3):548-555.

- PMID: 39313675
- PMCID: PMC11735929
- DOI: 10.1038/s41386-024-01996-y
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39313675/

## Design
- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled two-day fMRI study.
- N = 124 healthy, naturally cycling females not taking hormonal contraceptives.
- Behavioral, SCR, fMRI, cortisol and self-report outcomes.
- Oxytocin/placebo manipulated across fear-conditioning and extinction-learning days.

## Intervention as studied
- 24 IU intranasal oxytocin.
- Approximately 45 min before the relevant fear-conditioning/extinction procedure.

## Main result
The study found that intranasal oxytocin:
- did not materially improve fear acquisition;
- impaired extinction learning when exposure occurred after oxytocin;
- was associated with greater SCR and altered anterior-insula/temporal activity;
- did not include the kind of delayed extinction-recall phase needed to establish whether the impairment persisted.

The authors explicitly concluded that, at the present stage of evidence, oxytocin could not be recommended as an exposure-therapy adjunct.

## FearPrime interpretation
This is strong evidence against treating oxytocin as a generic “fear extinction enhancer.”

A more defensible model is:
`OT effect = sex × social/context salience × timing × endpoint × paradigm`

## Debug / limitations
- Female-only healthy sample.
- No delayed extinction-recall test.
- Single dose, so no dose-response inference.
- Differences from Acheson 2013 may reflect sex, endpoint, paradigm, timing or sampling rather than a simple contradiction.

## FearPrime weight
**High.** Oxytocin remains a research candidate, but currently with high uncertainty and directionally mixed human evidence.
