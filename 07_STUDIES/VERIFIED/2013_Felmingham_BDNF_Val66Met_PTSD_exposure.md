# Felmingham et al. 2013 — BDNF Val66Met predicts exposure-therapy response in PTSD

**Status:** VERIFIED HUMAN CLINICAL STUDY  
**Evidence grade:** A/B translational-clinical  
**Track:** BDNF / TrkB / extinction capacity

## Citation
Felmingham KL, Dobson-Stone C, Schofield PR, Quirk GJ, Bryant RA. *The brain-derived neurotrophic factor Val66Met polymorphism predicts response to exposure therapy in posttraumatic stress disorder.* Biological Psychiatry. 2013;73(11):1059-1063.

- PMID: 23312562
- PMCID: PMC4185184
- DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsych.2012.10.033
- Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23312562/

## Design
- N = 55 patients with PTSD.
- 8-week exposure-based CBT program.
- Val/Val: n = 30.
- Met-66 carriers: n = 25.
- BDNF genotype tested as a predictor of reduction in PTSD severity.

## Main result
Met-66 carriers showed poorer response to exposure therapy than Val/Val carriers. Pretreatment PTSD severity and BDNF Val66Met genotype predicted treatment response.

## FearPrime interpretation
This provides direct clinical evidence that **plasticity biology can moderate exposure outcome**.

It supports the model:
`exposure learning quality × endogenous plasticity capacity → treatment retention/generalization`

It does not prove that pharmacologically increasing BDNF will necessarily improve PTSD. BDNF also participates in acquisition and stabilization of fear memories.

## Debug
- Modest sample size.
- Genetic association is not a randomized manipulation of BDNF.
- Population-level genotype associations should not be used as deterministic predictions for an individual.
- Exposure response is a clinical endpoint, not a direct assay of TrkB signaling.

## FearPrime weight
**High translational relevance.**
