# Testosterone — human fear effects versus extinction evidence

**Status:** VERIFIED HUMAN FEAR EFFECTS; DIRECT EXTINCTION-AUGMENTATION EVIDENCE NOT ESTABLISHED  
**Evidence grade:** B for acute fear modulation; D/E for extinction augmentation  
**Track:** androgens / threat control

## van Honk / Hermans et al. 2005

**Title:** Testosterone reduces unconscious fear but not consciously experienced anxiety: implications for the disorders of fear and anxiety

- PMID: 15939408
- Double-blind placebo-controlled crossover.
- N = 16 healthy women.
- Single 0.5 mg testosterone administration.

### Main result
Testosterone reduced an automatic/vigilant response to masked fearful faces while self-reported anxiety was unchanged.

Primary:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15939408/

## Hermans et al. 2006

**Title:** A single administration of testosterone reduces fear-potentiated startle in humans

- PMID: 16458259
- DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.11.015
- N = 20 healthy women.
- Double-blind placebo-controlled crossover.
- Fear-potentiated startle measured in a verbal threat-of-shock paradigm.

### Main result
Testosterone reduced fear-potentiated startle without reducing baseline startle.

Primary:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16458259/

## Threat/escape network evidence

**Title:** Effects of testosterone administration on threat and escape anticipation in the orbitofrontal cortex

- PMID: 29902666
- N = 22 women.
- Within-subject placebo-controlled administration.
- Testosterone altered neural processing during escapable/inescapable threat.

Primary:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29902666/

## FearPrime interpretation

There is direct human evidence that testosterone can alter **fear expression/threat processing**.

However, this does not establish:
- improved extinction acquisition,
- stronger extinction consolidation,
- improved next-day extinction recall,
- reduced renewal,
- reduced reinstatement,
- or improved PTSD exposure-therapy outcome.

Therefore testosterone must not be ranked with butyrate, L-DOPA, DCS, FAAH inhibition or exercise as a demonstrated extinction augmenter.

## Key distinction

`less acute fear expression ≠ stronger extinction memory`

Acute fear suppression could even complicate interpretation if reduced arousal makes prediction error or exposure quality harder to quantify.

## FearPrime weight
**Mechanistically interesting fear-state modifier; extinction role unproven.**
